# Best overall registered agent for a Texas LLC

Texas makes forming an LLC cheap on paper. The state charges $300, the form is short, and you can file online in one sitting. Then people hit the part nobody warns them about: the registered agent.

Texas will not let your LLC be its own agent. Your agent has to be a Texas resident or a registered entity, with a street address where legal papers can be handed to a real person during business hours. A mailbox does not count. Miss that detail and your filing gets rejected, or worse, a lawsuit lands on a desk nobody checks.

## How to Start an LLC in Texas, Step by Step

Before comparing agents, here is the whole process. It is shorter than you think.

### Step 1: Choose a name

Your name must be distinguishable from other entities on file with the Texas Secretary of State, and it must include a designator such as "LLC" or "Limited Liability Company." Search the state's records before you fall in love with a name. If you plan to operate under a different brand, you will register an assumed name separately.

### Step 2: Choose your registered agent

This is the step that trips people up. The [Texas Secretary of State's instructions for Form 205](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/corp/instructions/205.shtml) say the agent must be a Texas resident, a domestic entity, or a foreign entity registered in Texas. The LLC cannot be its own agent. The registered office must be a street address where the agent can be personally served during normal business hours, not solely a mailbox or an answering service. The agent also has to consent, in writing or electronically.

You have two realistic options:

- **Be your own agent.** It costs nothing, but your name and home address become part of the public record, and you must be reachable at that address during business hours.
- **Hire a commercial agent.** You pay a yearly fee, and the agent's address goes on the public record instead of yours.

If you work from home, travel, or want your home address off a public database, a commercial agent usually makes sense.

### Step 3: File the Certificate of Formation

The document is Form 205. The filing fee is $300. You can file online through SOSPortal, where a credit card adds a convenience fee of $0.25 plus 2.25 percent of the total, or by mail or in person with a check or money order. Online filing is the fastest route.

### Step 4: Write an operating agreement

Texas does not require you to file one with the state, but you should have one. It records who owns what percentage, who makes decisions, how profits are split, and what happens if someone leaves. Single-member LLCs benefit too, because it shows you treat the company as a separate entity.

### Step 5: Get your EIN

The IRS issues the Employer Identification Number for free. Apply on the [IRS website](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number). Some formation sites charge for this, and you never need to pay for it. Under the IRS default rules, a single-member LLC is disregarded for income tax and a multi-member LLC is taxed as a partnership, so the EIN mostly matters for banking, hiring, and filing.

### Step 6: Handle Texas tax filings

Texas has no personal income tax, but LLCs generally have a franchise tax report obligation with the Comptroller, even when no tax is due. Third-party guides put the due date at May 15 each year. Check the Comptroller's [franchise tax page](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/franchise/) for the current deadline and threshold. This one is yours to file. No agent does it for you.

### Step 7: Open a business bank account

With your filed Certificate of Formation, EIN, and operating agreement, open an account in the LLC's name. Keeping personal and business money apart protects the liability shield you formed the LLC to get.

## Why the Agent Decision Matters More Than It Looks

A registered agent receives service of process, which means lawsuits, subpoenas, and official notices from the state. If the agent is unavailable or the address is stale, the LLC can miss a deadline to respond. The consequence can be a default judgment.

So the question is not "what is the cheapest agent?" It is "what is the cheapest agent I can trust to keep working?" Three things separate providers.

**The renewal price.** A promotional first-year price that rises at renewal is common. Over three years, ZenBusiness's $99 opener ends up $122 above Northwest's flat $375 total. That is the number to compare, not the first-year price.

**The extra fees.** Several providers charge for things people assume are included: mail scanning past a limit, long legal documents, a mailing address, or a switch away from a prior agent. Ask for these in writing before you pay.

**The exit.** Prepay plans lower the yearly cost but are non-refundable. If you might dissolve the LLC or change agents, the cheaper plan can be the more expensive one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a registered agent to form an LLC in Texas?

Yes. Every Texas LLC must name a registered agent and a registered office in its Certificate of Formation. The LLC cannot act as its own agent.

### Can I be my own registered agent in Texas?

Yes, if you are a Texas resident with a street address where you can be personally served during normal business hours. Your name and address then appear on the public record. Many owners prefer a commercial registered agent for privacy and reliability.

### What is the difference between a registered agent and a statutory agent?

They mean the same thing. Texas uses "registered agent," while some states use "statutory agent" or "resident agent." The role is to receive service of process and official state mail for the LLC.

### How much does it cost to form an LLC in Texas?

The state filing fee for the Certificate of Formation (Form 205) is $300. Filing online through SOSPortal adds a credit card convenience fee. A commercial registered agent is an additional yearly cost, with the five providers above listing $99 to $199 a year depending on the provider and the year.

### Can I use a P.O. box or a virtual office as my registered office?

No. The Secretary of State says the registered office must be a street address where the agent can be personally served, and it cannot be solely a mailbox or a telephone answering service.

### What happens if my registered agent resigns or I miss a change?

You need a registered agent on file at all times. If you switch, file a change of registered agent with the Secretary of State and keep the old agent active until the state confirms the change. Texas lists a $15 fee for that filing.

### Do I need an operating agreement for a Texas LLC?

Texas does not require you to file one, but you should write one. It documents ownership percentages, management, profit splits, and what happens when a member leaves, and it supports your claim that the LLC is a separate entity.

### How do I get an EIN for a Texas LLC?

Apply with the IRS for free. If the responsible party has a Social Security number or ITIN, the online application issues the number immediately. Never pay a formation site for one.

### Does a Texas LLC owe franchise tax?

Texas LLCs generally have an annual franchise tax report obligation with the Comptroller, even when no tax is due. Third-party guides put the due date at May 15 each year. Check the Comptroller's website for the current threshold and deadline.

### How long does it take to form an LLC in Texas?

Online filing through SOSPortal is usually the fastest route, and mailed filings take longer. Processing times change, so check the Secretary of State's current guidance. Our [guide to LLC approval times](https://persuasion-nation.com/how-long-does-it-take-to-get-an-llc/) covers the ranges by state and filing method.

### Should I form my Texas business in another state like Wyoming or Delaware?

Usually not. If you operate in Texas, Texas treats an out-of-state LLC as a foreign LLC and charges $750 to register it, on top of the other state's fees. For most owners, forming at home is the simpler and cheaper route.

### What does a registered agent do besides accept lawsuits?

An agent receives service of process, government notices, and official state correspondence, and forwards them to you. Some providers also scan mail, send compliance reminders, and offer business addresses for an added fee.

### Can I change registered agents later?

Yes. File a change of registered agent with the Secretary of State, get the new agent's consent, and cancel the old service only after the state confirms the filing. Some providers charge a fee to switch.
